    Four rules behind the signals

    What changed was the filter, not the setups.

    01

    Not every breakout is a buy or sell

    The ORB needs a clear directional bias, a close outside the opening range, displacement, and a 1-minute FVG confirming the move. If the market is choppy or there's no clear draw on liquidity, it's a pass.

    Choppy or no DOL → NO TRADE
    02

    Backtest the conditions, not just the entries

    The same general market gave very different results depending on what was being tested. A setup that looks good on a chart doesn't automatically have an edge across different conditions.

    Tested conditions → signal
    03

    Doing nothing is a valid signal

    Some weeks price fits the setups well. Other weeks there's barely anything worth risking money on. Forcing trades in slow periods is the habit to break. Missing a move is fine.

    No setup → stay flat
    04

    Judge execution, not P&L

    A green trade taken outside the rules is still a bad trade. Review entries, risk, timing, and whether the setup actually met the criteria. Track all of your data.

    Off-plan winner = bad trade
